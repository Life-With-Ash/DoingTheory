Motivations

the why, what I use, and who inspires me.

Disclaimer - I know my writing isn't the best, most professional things that's ever existed. That's kind of the point. I want to provide access. I am also doing this for fun, and while I will always prioritize academic and jounarlistic integrity, I think there are more accessible ways to do that than APA 7.

I’ve tried to do something like this many times. At first, I really didn’t want to learn a new coding language, so I looked for a free website-making service. It was important to me that it was free because I'm a student (I kind of don’t have money for that). I’d rather get coffee with oat milk, yk? Also, I think it should be free for me to do this stuff. Like, I just want to put my thoughts out into the world without being controlled by a social media algorithm or limited by a third-party site builder. After a lot of searching (and still being too stubborn to learn HTML), I gave up.

A few years later, I left for college and wanted to keep up with my friends. So, I started a newsletter. In my usual fashion, I got one email in, and realized I could do better. I spent the next month or so researching what it would take for me to build a website. This is where I begrudgingly decided to learn HTML.

The first coding language I learned was Java, in CS 180 at Western Kentucky University, residential dual enrollment through the Gatton Academy (it never felt as fancy as it sounds). I can say pretty confidently that I did well. I also liked it a lot more than I thought I would. Computer Science had always been advertised to me as a very masculine space, and it is. But if you do a few seconds of looking, you’ll stumble upon a really amazing community of women and queer computer science engineers.

Actually, they are responsible for some of the most important CS advancements. Like Ada Lovelace, the first-ever computer programmer, or Margaret Hamilton, a NASA software engineer. Because of those women, now there are people turning replica Sailor Moon compacts into cyberdecks (mini DIY computers). There are also super cool organizations like Girls Who Code, bringing CS to even more people.

I find that feminists do computer science differently. They do it the way I learned HTML. Figuring it out ourselves. If the rest of the field wasn’t going to welcome us, we would make our own space for learning.

That’s what I mean when I say “feminism is an act of taking up space”. It’s not just pushing through a crowd to make room for yourself at a concert. It’s also starting your own band and telling the 6-foot guy at barricade to get to the back, so that all the people who usually have to push and shove (or just give up) can have the experience they bought a ticket for. I don’t know if that metaphor works for other brains. It works for mine.

So, this website is my way of taking up space.

Resources

The most important thing to me in this project is making it accessible and free. So, for anyone else who wants to do something like this, here are the resources I used and how I used them:

Resource What it's for
Visual Studio Code This is where you will build and edit your code.
Live Server, Ritwick Dey This extension can be added to VS Code to allow you to view your edits live, very helpful when you’re working out bugs.
Homebrew Necessary for installation of VS Code and some terminal interactions, there’s some other services that do the same thing, but this is what I used.
Github Pages and Repositories This is how you can publish your site, it is completely free as long as you are publishing something public.
HTML5 UP They have a bunch of great html templates that you can use to start out, I used the Massively template, just make sure you give credit!
Google / Search Engine of Choice This is honestly my most used resource, I am constantly searching for what different functions and terms mean, or ways to include functions I don’t know how to write yet. There are tons of people on the internet who write about how to do html, make use of that free resource.
CSS-Tricks I use Docs to draft the things I write before publishing. I use Sheets and App Scripts to manage my email subscribers. I also use Gmail for managing my Github account, contacting people and more.
Instagram I use instagram to advertise and contact people I want to write about.
GIMP I use this software to edit photos and do some graphic design, FYI: it is pixel based and cannot do vectors, but it’s free and regularly updated.
Google Fonts This is where I get the different fonts I use in my CSS.

As I find more resources, I will expand this list!
